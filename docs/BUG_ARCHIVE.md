# 🐞 BUG_ARCHIVE.md - Resolved Skate Physics & Architecture Bugs

This document serves as an archival repository of past bug investigations, post-mortems, and resolved regressions in **Skate Sim V2**. AI agents and contributors should consult this document when investigating new regressions, unexpected behavior in rotation physics, or Switch/Fakie sign inconsistencies.

---

## ✅ Resolved Bugs & Post-Mortems

### 1. Flip Tricks Unlandable on Any Raised Surface
- **Symptom:** Reported as "kickflip into a manual on the curb stalls every time"; the manual was incidental, a plain kickflip onto the curb bailed identically.
- **Root Cause:** `is_flip_in_progress` cleared only after the deck swept a full $360^\circ$ at `flip_speed_deg`, a **fixed 0.60 s wall-clock duration** regardless of hang time — while hang time *shrinks with the height gained from takeoff surface to landing surface*. Popping onto the `y = 0.30` curb gave 35 frames against 36 needed: one frame short, deterministically. `_evaluate_touchdown_landing()` hit the primo branch and `return`ed **before** the manual-zone check, which is why it presented as "flip tricks break manuals".
- **Resolution:** Landing is now judged on the deck's orientation against `catch_cone_deg = min(atan(grip_friction), rail-strike angle)` — the friction cone a shoe can stamp an off-axis deck flat within, clamped by the roll angle at which the deck's rail would reach the ground before its wheels. Nothing in the landing path reads airtime, ledge height, gravity, or frame rate any longer.

### 2. 360-Spin Tricks Finished Yaw Before Roll
- **Symptom:** A tre flip's yaw ran at `spin_speed_deg * 2` (0.42 s) while its roll ran at `flip_speed_deg` (0.59 s), so the deck visibly stopped spinning at roughly $253^\circ$ of flip and kept rolling alone before halting.
- **Root Cause:** Independent fixed-rate animations with no shared notion of the trick as one physical event.
- **Resolution:** Fixed by `_impart_deck_rotation()`. Rotation is now imparted once at pop as a constant angular velocity, with both axes scaled onto one shared trick duration so roll and yaw always retire on the same frame.

### 3. "Slide" Stayed High on HUD Long After Landing
- **Symptom:** Read as wheel grip failing to scrub off drift after landing.
- **Root Cause:** **Not a physics bug at all** — the readout was mislabelled. `last_landing_slide` is a *latched* sample taken once inside `_evaluate_touchdown_landing()` and held until the next touchdown; nothing clears it, deliberately, because its whole job is to let `max_landing_slide` be tuned against a landing after the fact. Labelled bare `Slide` next to the live `Speed` it read as live state, so a persisting landing figure looked exactly like lateral velocity that never decayed. Instrumented headlessly on a $20^\circ$ landing at 5 m/s: live lateral speed fell `1.68 → 0.10 m/s` in 20 frames and to exactly zero by frame 30 (**0.5 s**) while the latched value sat at `1.682` for the full 3 s probe.
- **Resolution:** Fixed in the HUD only: split into `Slip` (live, from read-only `lateral_speed`) and `Last slide` (latched). The grip integrator was correct and left untouched.

### 4. Switch/Fakie Sign & Mirror Errors (Recurring Bug Class)
- **Warning:** Switch/Fakie sign errors are this project's recurring bug class — do not assume "validated" means immune.
- **History:** Three separate ones surfaced in a single session (reversed ankle pegs, inverted shuv naming, inverted body-rotation naming), each found by *play*, not by tests, because each fails silently as a mirror image rather than a hard crash or runtime error.
- **Rule of Thumb:** When touching anything rotational, always check it in all four of `{regular, goofy} × {forward, switch/fakie}` before calling a fix complete.

### 5. Manual Drop-Out During Shove-it Scoops & Over-Pitch Landing Bails
- **Symptom:** Attempting to scoop a Shove-it, Varial Flip, or Tre Flip out of an active manual caused the manual to abruptly drop to flat pavement right before takeoff. Similarly, slamming down for a hard popped kickflip or holding a deep pop lock past $0.90$ upon touchdown triggered an accidental wheel drop or over-pitch landing bail ("BAIL! (Wheel Bite / Over-Pitched)").
- **Root Cause:** Both grounded pitch (`_apply_grounded_board_pitch`) and landing judging (`_evaluate_touchdown_landing`) enforced an artificial vertical Cartesian window: `back.y <= 0.90`. During a Shove-it scoop, thumbsticks sweep horizontally around the joystick rim, causing vertical Cartesian $Y$ to drop below $0.20$ while total vector magnitude remains strong ($\ge 0.80$). During a heavy pop setup or deep touchdown landing hold, pushing the stick fully to $1.0$ exceeded the $0.90$ ceiling, prompting the engine to erroneously declare the skater had stopped manualing.
- **Resolution:** Eliminated the artificial $\le 0.90$ ceiling and aligned manual detection with our radial polar input model. Manual balance now checks vector magnitude (`length() \ge 0.20`) during active pop loading (`LOADING_OLLIE`/`LOADING_NOLLIE`), smoothly maintaining truck pivot tilt during full circular scoops and deep pop deflections without dropping out or clashing with touchdown landing tolerances.

### 6. Kickturn Pivoted on the Airborne Truck in Switch Stance
- **Symptom:** Reported first as "after landing a trick with a 180 body rotation and coming to a stop, sometimes the truck in the air is the pivot point and the grounded truck orbits around it", then narrowed by the reporter to "it happens in switch stance **all the time**." That second observation is what identified it — "sometimes after a 180" and "always in switch" are the same condition.
- **Root Cause:** A **frame mismatch**, the project's recurring bug class. `_apply_steering()` picked the kickturn anchor axle from `input_state.leading_foot`, which is resolved against `BoardPivot`'s basis — and `BoardPivot` carries the 0/180 switch-stance flip. But the rotation and the anchor lookup (`rotate_y`, `to_global`) both happen on `SkaterRoot`, whose yaw does **not** carry that flip. Whenever the pivot sat at 180° the Z sign inverted, so the code anchored the *leading* axle. Since `_apply_grounded_board_pitch()` lifts the leading trucks for a kickturn (`kickturn_pitch_deg`), the anchor was precisely the truck in the air. `board_pivot.rotation_degrees.y` is 180 for all of switch and fakie, and `_evaluate_touchdown_landing()` keeps the nearest multiple of 180 on the pivot after a landed body-180 — hence both reported presentations.
  - Notably `_apply_manual_pivot()` had always been correct, multiplying through `Basis(Vector3.UP, board_pivot.rotation.y)`. The two functions silently disagreed about which axle was on the ground.
- **Resolution:** Carry the flip across explicitly: `rear_axle_z *= (1.0 if cos(board_pivot.rotation.y) >= 0.0 else -1.0)`. Measured before and after in `tests/carve_and_push.tscn`: the grounded axle swung **0.940 m** while the airborne one moved 0.041 m; afterwards switch reproduces the forward case exactly (`lead 0.860 / trail 0.041`).
- **Process note:** Three other mechanisms were hypothesised first and all three were **disproven by measurement** — a latched `_realigning` weakening grip, a kickturn position teleport, and carve axis drift. Those probes are retained as cases in `tests/carve_and_push.gd` so the disproven theories stay disproven. The bug was only found once the reporter supplied the switch-stance detail; no amount of reasoning about the carve maths would have reached it.

### 7. Landing Skid — Holding a Trigger Through a Touchdown Slid the Board Sideways
- **Symptom:** "Upon landing, if you hold down a trigger, you can start skidding in that direction." Reproduced at **89.8° off the rolling axis** — travelling essentially sideways — with grip left capped for 127 of 150 frames.
- **Root Cause:** A **positive feedback loop the rider drove directly.** `_realigning` was a boolean latch set at every touchdown; while true it capped wheel grip at `speed * landing_turn_rate_deg` (~7 m/s² against `wheel_side_grip`'s 40) so a crooked landing would swing into line smoothly rather than snapping. It cleared only when live lateral speed fell under `0.02 m/s`. But **steering generates lateral speed**, and at full lean it generates it at ~21 m/s² — faster than the capped grip removes it. So holding a trigger through a landing meant the clear condition never arrived: weak grip kept lateral high, and high lateral kept grip weak.
- **The flaw underneath:** one number could not distinguish two different things — sideways speed *left over* from the landing (should be paced, for smoothness) from sideways speed the rider is *creating right now* by carving (should meet full grip).
- **Resolution:** Replaced the latch with `_landing_residual`, a **budget that only ever shrinks**. It is armed once, at touchdown, with the sideways speed actually arrived with, and drains at the paced rate. Grip removes everything *above* the budget at full strength. At touchdown lateral equals the budget, so the residual works off at exactly `landing_turn_rate_deg` and the landing feels unchanged; lateral the rider adds by steering sits above the budget and is scrubbed immediately. Nothing the rider does can raise the budget, so the loop cannot form.
- **Process note — a wrong test is worse than no test.** This exact mechanism was hypothesised first and **wrongly dismissed**: the disproving test carved after a *straight* landing (residual ≈ 0, so the latch cleared on frame 1 before the carve began), and its one counter-steer case was paired with a 40° landing that bailed to zero speed and measured nothing. The suite reported `realigning 6/120` and the theory was recorded as disproven in the test's own header. It took the user finding the precise input combination — real residual, survivable speed, steering *away* from it — to reopen it. When a test clears a hypothesis, check that the test actually reaches the state the hypothesis is about.
