## 🛹 Project Vision & Control Philosophy
**Skate Sim V2** is an authentic, physics-driven skateboarding simulator built in Godot 4.7 (Vulkan Forward+), designed around anatomical foot control, weighted physical responsiveness, and state-of-the-art procedural animation.

### Core Control Tenets
- **Anatomical Foot Mapping:** Analog thumbsticks correspond directly to physical feet:
  - **Left Stick = Left Foot** (Blue shoe box)
  - **Right Stick = Right Foot** (Red shoe box)
- **Universal Trick Rule:** Whether riding **Regular Forward, Switch, Nollie, or Fakie**:
  - Controls always map directly to the foot physically sitting over the active nose or tail.
  - **Flics Outward** (away from toes, behind the body plane) = **Kickflip**.
  - **Flices Inward** (toward toes, in front of the body plane) = **Heelflip**.
- **Scoop & Shove-it Symmetry:** The skateboard kicks directly in the true rotational direction of your thumbstick sweep, completely independent of starting position on the joystick or physical deck alignment.
  - **Standard Scoop Buffer ($40^\circ \to 94^\circ$ arc span):** Triggers a **$180^\circ$ Pop Shove-it** (or Varial Flip).
  - **Deep Scoop Buffer ($\ge 95^\circ$ arc span):** Triggers a **$360^\circ$ Pop Shove-it** (or 360 Flip / Tre Flip, Laser Flip), reducing required thumbstick rotational effort while avoiding accidental misfires.
- **Analog Trigger Body Rotation:** L and R trigger pulls spin the skater in mid-air. An **audio-log power taper curve ($y = \text{sign}(x) \cdot |x|^{2.2}$)** desensitizes light-to-mid squeezes for subtle styling adjustments while scaling exponentially to 100% spin velocity (`554 deg/s`) for explosive flat-ground 360s!

## 🛠️ Key Files & System Architecture

### 1. `res://scripts/player/SkaterController.gd` (3-Layer Kinematics & Touchdown Engine)
- **Layer 1 (Body Spin Authority):** Trigger lean rotates `board_pivot.y` directly (with fluid angular momentum lerping `lerp 20.0 * delta`) so the rolling travel vector and chase camera stay anchored cleanly behind the rider. Pre-wind steering on the ground is damped by 80% during pop loading.
- **Layer 2 (Airborne & Grounded Pitch Control):** Thumbsticks tilt the nose/tail between `0.20 to 0.90` stick deflection for manuals ($24^\circ$ tilt) and airborne realignments.
- **Layer 3 (Deck Flip & Spin Authority):** `board_mesh.z` (roll) and `board_mesh.y` (yaw) rotate cleanly toward targets. Shoe hover catching raises shoe boxes to $Y=0.18\text{m}$ during flips, locking in as griptape revolves face up. Both shoe coordinates firmly snap back to deck rest pose immediately upon touchdown and maintain locked standing posture during grounded rolling whenever not executing a foot push stroke.
- **Touchdown Tolerances:**
  - **Manual Catch Zone:** Landing within $\pm 15^\circ$ pitch while holding middle stick zone ($0.20 \to 0.90$) bypasses entry delays for instant, silky manual rollouts.
  - **Sideways Landing Window ($\pm 45^\circ$):** Touching down within $\pm 45^\circ$ of straight ($0^\circ/180^\circ$) snaps to orientation. Landing sideways perpendicular to travel direction ($45^\circ \to 135^\circ$ or $225^\circ \to 315^\circ$) fires a `"BAIL! (Sideways Landing / Wheel Skid)"` speed crash!
- **Simulation Speed Calibration:** Cruising max speed is calibrated to `7.0 m/s` ($\approx 15.7\text{ mph}$) with `2.0 m/s` foot pushes and `608 deg/s` flip rotation speeds for readable, floaty trick execution.

### 2. `res://scripts/input/FootInputState.gd` (Input Classification & Trick Nomenclature)
- **Stance Tracking:** Dynamically evaluates `leading_foot` and `trailing_foot` using travel velocity dot product when moving, or the **`SkaterRoot` forward vector passed in as `root`** when stationary. This ensures accurate switch foot recognition even when standing completely still. (It formerly reached the yaw frame via `pivot.get_parent()`; that now resolves to the surface-tilted `SurfaceAlign` and would skew the result on any ramp — see the rule in section 2b.)
- **Foot identity is an `enum`, not a String.** `Foot { LEFT, RIGHT }` and `DeckEnd { NOSE, TAIL }`. Compare enum values; convert to text **only** at the HUD boundary via `foot_name()` / `deck_end_name()`. The old `begins_with("Left")` string tests were the same silent-failure shape as the trick-name strings already removed from this project.
- **`front_stick()` / `back_stick()`** return the leading / trailing foot's stick. Use them rather than re-deriving stance handedness inline — that block was duplicated five times across two files.
- **Arc Sweep Measurement (`max_swept_angle`):** Accumulates frame-to-frame `angle_difference` deltas **in degrees** (`accumulated_scoop_deg`) during pop loading (`LOADING_OLLIE`/`LOADING_NOLLIE`) to separate quarter-circle from half-circle scoops.
- **Trick Measurement:** `_build_trick_signature()` populates `current_trick` (a `TrickSignature`) with pop / flip / shuv. It builds **no display strings** — naming happens at touchdown in `SkaterController._finalise_trick_name()`.
- **Push Grammar:** `Mongo Push (Leading)` / `Standard Push (Trailing)` classification remains string-based (display only).

### 2a. 🏷️ Trick Naming (`res://scripts/tricks/`)
- **`TrickSignature.gd`** — measurement of what physically happened: `pop`, `flip`, `shuv_deg` (board **relative to the body**), `body_deg`, plus derived `board_world_deg` (= body + shuv), `body_with_shuv`, `lands_switch`. `describe()` returns the HUD readout.
- **`TrickNames.gd`** — the editable `TABLE` and `resolve()`. **This is the only file to edit to add or rename a trick.**
- **CORE PRINCIPLE:** the engine **encodes no skateboarding naming conventions**. It never decides what is frontside, whether fakie reverses labels, or whether nollie keeps them. It measures the action; the table assigns the name. Skate naming has too many one-off rules (reference material consulted during design contradicted itself on the fakie case) for correctness to depend on the engine interpreting them.
- **Authoring workflow:** do the trick, read the `Signature:` line off the DebugHUD, paste those values into `TABLE` with the name you want. No reasoning about rotation conventions required.
- **RULE — count spin in `world`, not `shuv`, when authoring rows.** `shuv` is board rotation *relative to the body*; `board_world` (= shuv + body) is what the board actually turns. A trick described as "360 degrees of spin" is 360 of **world** rotation, and a simultaneous 180 body rotation supplies half of it — so a **Bigflip** is `shuv = -180, body = -180` (world -360), **not** `shuv = -360`. The whole bigspin family is one motion (board 360 world, body 180) with the flip deciding the name: no flip → Bigspin, kickflip → Bigflip, heelflip → Bigspin Heelflip.
- **RULE — unlisted fields must be inert.** In a rule, any of `flip` / `shuv` / `body` you omit **must be zero** to match. This stops `{shuv = -360, flip = KICK}` ("360 Flip") from silently swallowing a 360 flip that also had a body rotation. `pop` is the exception (omit it and the row covers all four stances with the stance added as a prefix); derived fields are opt-in; write `ANY` to explicitly ignore a field.
- **RULE — ⚠️ THE #1 SOURCE OF BUGS: the two rotations are measured in DIFFERENT FRAMES.** Every naming bug so far has been one rotation stored as if it were in the other's frame. The failure is silent: nothing crashes, the trick just resolves to its mirror image. Both conversions live in `TrickSignature.gd` and **nowhere else**:

| Quantity | Measured in | Already rider-relative? | Converter |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shuv_deg` | thumbstick sweep | **Yes** for facing (sticks map anatomically to feet) | `shuv_sign(is_goofy, left_foot_scoops)` — the scooping stick is **right** for Ollie/Fakie but **left** for Switch/Nollie, so an identical sweep means the opposite board direction |
| `body_deg` | **world** yaw of `board_pivot` | **No** | `body_sign(is_goofy, riding_reversed)` — riding switch/fakie puts the rider's frontside the other way round in the world |

  `riding_reversed` is sampled from the pivot's real yaw at pop (`cos(deg_to_rad(board_pivot.rotation_degrees.y)) < 0.0`, the same idiom as `deck_reversed`) rather than inferred from the pop type, and captured at pop because the yaw changes in flight.
- **RULE — the engine guarantees CONSISTENCY, not polarity.** Which direction is called "frontside" is a naming choice owned by `TABLE`. The invariant the code must hold is that one felt trick yields one signature across all stances and facings; `mirror_test` asserts exactly that, and additionally pins the regular/forward case so a future sign fix cannot silently invert an already-calibrated table.
- **RULE — naming happens at touchdown, never at pop.** Body rotation accrues *during flight* (`airborne_body_yaw_deg`), so tricks defined partly by body rotation (bigflip, frontside flip, body varial) cannot be named at pop. Mid-air status shows no trick name. Bails leave the previous landed trick on display.

### 2b. 🧱 Surface Collision (`res://scripts/physics/SurfaceProbe.gd`)
- **`SurfaceProbe.gd`** — pure geometry against the world's static collision: `cast_down`, `highest_below`, `cast_horizontal`, and `find_edge_near`. It knows **nothing** about skating, tricks or grinding, and must stay that way.
- **Uses direct space state queries, not `RayCast3D` nodes.** The old truck raycasts were children of `BoardPivot`, so they tilted with trick pitch ($22^\circ$ on every pop) and probed sideways exactly when landing accuracy mattered most. Explicit world-space `from`/`to` keeps probe direction true regardless of board rotation. The four `RayCast3D` nodes were deleted.
- Probe origins come from `SkaterRoot`'s **yaw-only** basis (`_truck_points()`), for the same reason.
- **Spline-free edge detection:** an edge is the intersection of two faces, so its direction is the cross product of their normals — down-cast for the ledge top, sideways cast just beneath it for the side face, then `top_normal.cross(side_normal)`. Works on any convex edge of any static body, needs no authoring, and re-probing each tick follows segmented or curved edges naturally.
- **RULE — ⚠️ SURFACE TILT LIVES ON ITS OWN NODE (`SurfaceAlign`).** The rig is `SkaterRoot → SurfaceAlign → BoardPivot → BoardMesh`. `SurfaceAlign` carries **only** surface pitch/roll. It must never be merged into `BoardPivot.rotation_degrees.x`, which is trick pitch — written by the manual/pop systems and *read back* by `_evaluate_touchdown_landing` for manual-catch and wheel-bite checks — nor into `BoardMesh.rotation_degrees.z`, which is flip roll and is read by the naming system. Sharing a channel silently corrupts all of them.
- `CameraPivot` stays parented to `SkaterRoot`, so the chase camera does not roll with ramps.
- **`ride_height = 0.078`** is the measured distance from the rig origin to the lowest wheel vertex. It is **not** the old hardcoded `0.25` — that number was merely the height the rig happened to be placed at, while the `Ground` body's top surface is at `y = 0` (transform `y = -0.1`, box height `0.2`). Those two were never reconciled, so the board floated `17 cm` from the start of the project; with no real collision and no contact reference nobody noticed, and the detached shadow is what finally exposed it. If the deck model is ever swapped, re-measure this by finding the lowest wheel vertex in world space rather than guessing.
- **RULE — no hardcoded ground heights.** Ride height is `ride_height` (`0.25`, the rig origin-to-wheel offset). The former literals — touchdown at `y <= 0.25`, the `is_grounded` override `or (global_position.y <= 0.45)`, and the `> 0.27` / `> 0.60` thresholds — all assumed a flat world at y=0 and misfire on a curb. Ledge drop-off needs no special case: the probe simply finds nothing and the skater becomes airborne.
- **RULE — ⚠️ assert surface alignment as `board up axis == surface normal`, never as an angle magnitude.** `_apply_surface_alignment()` solves for the rotation carrying the board's local $+Y$ onto the probed normal: about X it maps $+Y \to (0, \cos t, \sin t)$ so `target.x = atan2(n.z, n.y)`; about Z it maps $+Y \to (-\sin t, \cos t, 0)$ so `target.z = atan2(-n.x, n.y)`. Getting either negation backwards tilts the board *away* from the slope by the slope angle rather than onto it — a **2x error** that reads in play as "pitches forward going up a ramp".
  - The original settle test compared `absf(surface_align.rotation.x)` to $15^\circ$, which passes whether the board tilts onto the slope or away from it, so it **could not detect a sign error by construction**. `align_test` now asserts the real invariant — the board's up axis must equal the surface normal — across six headings, plus that the uphill end actually rises.
- **RULE — ⚠️ manuals pivot on the contact axle, not the rig origin.** `BoardPivot` sits at the board centre, `ride_height` above the ground, so rotating it drove the dipping end straight through the floor (`8.7 cm` under at a full $24^\circ$ manual). `_apply_manual_pivot()` re-seats the board by offsetting `board_pivot.position`: rotating a child about a point `P` instead of its origin solves to `position = Ry * (P - Rx * P)`, so the body-spin yaw carries the offset around correctly. Applied **only while grounded** — airborne the deck must pitch about its centre or flips look wrong.
  - `P` is the **axle CENTRE**: `z = ±manual_axle_z (0.225)`, `y = -(ride_height - wheel_radius)`. The wheel is round, so pivoting at its centre keeps it tangent as it rolls; pivoting at the contact patch instead lifts the board a few mm at full tilt.
  - **Do not derive `manual_axle_z` from the wheels' bounding extents.** That yields `0.2489`, which is the axle centre *plus* the wheel radius — measure the front/rear vertex clusters separately and take each centre (`±0.2250`, a `0.45 m` wheelbase).
- **RULE — ⚠️ only the touchdown path may ground the skater.** Proximity to a surface may only *keep* `is_grounded` true; it must never flip it false→true. The airborne→grounded transition belongs exclusively to the touchdown branch in section 6, because that is what zeroes `vertical_velocity` and calls `_evaluate_touchdown_landing()` (landing tolerances, bail checks, trick naming). Allowing the section-1 proximity check to ground the skater skipped all of it whenever the snap band was entered gently — rolling off a low curb left `vertical_velocity` stuck negative forever and never resolved the trick name, while a fast drop from height masked the bug by passing through the band in a single frame.
- `update_stance_facts()` takes `SkaterRoot` explicitly as its `root` argument. It previously reached the yaw frame via `pivot.get_parent()`, which now resolves to the surface-tilted `SurfaceAlign` and would skew the stationary forward vector on any ramp.

### 3. `res://scripts/player/SkateDeckMesh.gd` (3D Skateboard Model & Material Engine)
- Extends `Node3D` and instantiates the standalone imported `res://skateboard.glb` asset (exported from `res://skateboard.blend` so contributors without a local Blender installation can load the project cleanly) to replace the former procedural placeholder deck.
- Applies precision scale calibration (`0.1722`) and centering offsets (`Vector3(-0.3239, -0.3585, 0.1169)`) to align directly with an 80 cm real-world deck sitting under the skater's shoes at `Y = 0.055m`.
- Features an inspector flag (`use_low_poly`, **default `true`**) to prevent Z-fighting between overlapping high/low poly meshes from Blender, and automatically assigns high-contrast, authentic skate materials (aluminum trucks, cream polyurethane wheels, and orange bushings).
- **LOD Tiers:** the high tier is **646,208 tris**, the low tier **8,328** — a `77.6x` difference that is visually indistinguishable at gameplay range, so the low tier is the shipping default. 65% of the high tier is spent on `bearings_high` (253,952) and `bolts_high` (164,928), parts that are sub-pixel at chase distance; `board_high` itself is only 79,040. Cause is Bevel + Subdivision modifiers baked at export on every `_high` object.
- **CAVEAT:** `use_low_poly` only controls `visible`, so both tiers are still instanced and **654,536 tris remain resident in memory**. The flag removes draw and shadow-pass cost, *not* load cost or the 22.7 MB `.scn` / 25.9 MB `.bin` import footprint. Reclaiming that needs the high tier stripped from the `.blend` or split into a separate asset.
- The low tier ships **no `TANGENT`** in the glTF, but `meshes/ensure_tangents=true` synthesizes them on import, so `deck_two_tone.gdshader` runs on it unmodified. The two tiers have inverted UV density (`board_low` is 2x denser across the width, ~half along the length) yet the grit reads identically — measured high-frequency RMS `7.5` vs `7.4` — so `grip_grain_scale` needs no per-tier retuning.
- **Two-Tone Deck (`res://resources/deck_two_tone.gdshader`):** The deck is driven by a `ShaderMaterial` rather than flat surface colors, exposing `grip_color`, `deck_bottom_color`, `bottom_emission`, `facing_sharpness`, and the `grip_grain_*` set as inspector properties.

### 3a. ⚠️ Deck Material Constraint: The Board Is One Surface
- `skateboard.blend` ships **no materials, no textures and no vertex colors** — the only material datablock is Blender's default `Dots Stroke` grease pencil, every mesh has `slots=0`, and the import uses placeholder materials (`blender/materials/export_materials=1`). All deck colour therefore lives in GDScript/shader code, **never** in the `.blend`.
- `board_high` exports 3 surfaces, but they do **not** map to grip/wood/edge. Surface 0 is the *entire* deck shell — top (area 4.98), bottom (area 5.03) and rails together — while surfaces 1-2 are hairline slivers (area 0.0098 and 0.0079) generated by stray out-of-range material indices (`28360`, `21808`) on a mesh with zero slots.
- **RULE:** Because top and bottom share one surface, they can never be separated with per-surface materials. The split is done per-fragment off the model-space normal (mesh-local **+Y is up**; the 180° Y node rotation on `board_high` leaves the up axis intact), so it rides along correctly through every flip, shuv and manual. All surfaces receive the same shader.
- The grit is deliberately faded out via `fwidth` once a noise cell drops below a pixel, so the deck reads as textured up close and as clean matte black at chase distance instead of aliasing into shimmer.

### 4. `res://scenes/player/SkaterRig.tscn` (Scene Tree & Visual Debugger)
- Contains `BoardPivot`, `BoardMesh`, truck contact raycasts, foot nodes (`LeftFoot`, `RightFoot`), and `CameraPivot`.
- Foot meshes are sized to roughly a **size US 11 M shoe** (`28.0 cm` length, `9.8 cm` width, `3.8 cm` height) with flush rounded sneaker toe tips (`ToeTip` cylinder child nodes at `X = 0.1155m`, radius `0.049m`).
- Features anatomical standing ankle poles (`PegPivot` shifted $-0.075\text{m}$ over heel articulation at `Y = 0.019m`) that stand upright vertically at rest and dynamically tilt up to `peg_tilt_deg` ($35^\circ$) during joystick deflections.
- Feet run along **local X** (toe at $+X$, heel at $-X$) and are seated at `X = -0.025m` so the heel edge pulls out over the heel rail and more of the toe sits on the deck — overhang is balanced at `0.043m` heel / `0.042m` toe (previously `0.018m` / `0.067m`).
- **RULE:** `left_foot_rest` / `right_foot_rest` are captured from this scene in `SkaterController._ready()`. Never re-hardcode them in script — duplicating the values silently reverted any scene-set foot offset the moment a push stroke finished.
- Chase camera sits closer at `Y = 1.25m, Z = 2.1m` behind the rider tilted down $25^\circ$ for intimate trick and deck visibility.
- **`AnklePeg` meshes are `cast_shadow = 0` (OFF).** They are debug visualisers, not part of the board: standing `0.283 m` up, at the scene's $60^\circ$ sun they threw shadows `163 mm` clear of the deck (offset is `height x 0.577`), reading as detached blobs disconnected from the board. Their shadows carry no information, so they are simply disabled.

### 4a. 💡 Shadow Tuning — Godot Defaults Are Wrong At This Scale
- **RULE:** the `DirectionalLight3D` must **not** run Godot's default shadow bias. The defaults (`shadow_bias = 0.1`, `shadow_normal_bias = 2.0`, `directional_shadow_max_distance = 100`) are calibrated for architectural scale; this project's parts are centimetres (deck `5.3 cm` thick, foot boxes `3.8 cm`, wheels `4.8 cm` diameter), so a normal bias of `2.0` displaces the shadow lookup far further than the geometry is thick. The result is textbook **peter-panning** — shadows detach from the objects casting them — plus blur from spreading the shadow atlas over 100 m.
- Tuned to `shadow_bias = 0.02`, `shadow_normal_bias = 0.1`, `directional_shadow_max_distance = 25.0`. The 25 m range covers gameplay comfortably (the chase camera sits `2.1 m` back) and buys a large increase in near-field shadow resolution.
- **Not every shadow oddity is a bug.** Shadow offset is `height x 0.577` at this sun angle, so the deck (`0.078 m` up) casts `45 mm` — mostly hidden beneath itself — while the feet cast `88 mm`. Some visible separation between board and foot shadows is geometrically correct; check the height maths before assuming a rendering fault.

## ⚠️ Critical Technical Notes & Architectural Rules
1. **Godot Euler Rotation Inversion ($\text{Y} = 180^\circ$ Compensation & Shove-it Decoupling):**
   - Because Godot uses `YXZ` Euler rotation order, turning the skateboard $180^\circ$ into Switch or Fakie flips the direction of the local X and Z axes relative to global world space.
   - **RULE:** Always apply stance sign compensation (`stance_sign = -1.0 if not left_is_front else 1.0`) when setting local X pitch targets for pops and manuals. For flip roll targets, decouple rotation from $180^\circ$ deck yaw reversals after Shove-its (`deck_orientation_comp = -1.0 if cos(deg_to_rad(board_mesh.y)) < 0.0 else 1.0`). In both Fakie and Nollie stance, because the flicking foot swaps ends while the rider's facing remains unchanged, mirror Kickflip/Heelflip trick labels directly in `FootInputState.gd` (`if (last_pop == TrickSignature.Pop.FAKIE_OLLIE or last_pop == TrickSignature.Pop.NOLLIE) ...`). This guarantees accurate HUD terminology while letting `stance_roll_sign` remain `1.0` without requiring artificial 3D roll inversions in `SkaterController.gd`. For Shove-it and Varial spin yaw targets around Y, NEVER couple rotation to `deck_orientation_comp` or physical deck reversal — the spin sign is simply `input_state.last_scoop_sign`, the raw rotational direction of the thumbstick sweep, matching the Core Tenet above.
2. **Modular Speed Parameters:**
   - Always derive motion rates from top-level exported variables (`flip_speed_deg`, `spin_speed_deg`, `body_spin_speed_deg`, `max_push_speed`, `push_impulse`, `rolling_friction`, `peg_tilt_deg`). Never hardcode velocity literals inside tick equations.
3. **Never Read a Display String to Drive Behaviour:**
   - Trick *physics* must key off `input_state.current_trick` (`sig.pop == …`, `absi(sig.shuv_deg) == 360`), never off `last_combo_string` or `active_spin_type`. Before this rule existed, `SkaterController` read `last_combo_string.contains("360")` / `.contains("Laser")` to pick spin degrees and spin velocity, so renaming a trick silently halved the board's spin speed.
   - **RULE:** `last_combo_string` and `last_pop_type` are **display only**. Regression-tested: a trick named `"360 Laser Flip"` carrying a 180 shuv still spins exactly 180.
   - ⚠️ `active_flip_type` is **not** display-only despite the name — it is the working variable inside `_build_trick_signature()` that the Fakie Kickflip/Heelflip swap mutates before it is matched into `sig.flip`. Deleting it as "cosmetic" silently breaks Fakie flip naming. (`active_spin_type` genuinely is dead: written, never read.)
4. **Screen-Space Debug Visualizers vs. `BoardPivot` Yaw:**
   - `CameraPivot` is parented to `SkaterRoot`, **not** `BoardPivot`, so it does not inherit the $180^\circ$ Switch/Fakie yaw. Any visualizer parented under `BoardPivot` that is meant to depict raw gamepad input therefore mirrors in switch (stick down reads as up, left as right).
   - **RULE:** Resolve the stick vector onto the pivot's local axes through `board_pivot.rotation.y` (`local_x = x*cos - y*sin`, `local_z = x*sin + y*cos`) rather than applying a stance sign flip. This stays correct at *any* yaw, so the pegs remain honest part-way through an aerial spin, not only at $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. Verified exact at $0/90/180/270^\circ$; composing the two Euler tilts leaves a harmless ~$3^\circ$ deviation at intermediate yaws such as $45^\circ$.
5. **`velocity` Is the Only Motion State — Never Rebuild It From Orientation:**
   - The line `velocity = -global_transform.basis.z * current_speed` made travel and facing **the same quantity**, so the skater could only ever move exactly where the board pointed. That single expression is why landings had to snap to a perfect $0/180^\circ$, why slope gravity could not be expressed at all, and why the sideways-landing test had nothing but an angle to judge with.
   - **RULE:** `velocity` is an authoritative world-space vector. `current_speed` is a **read-only** derived magnitude; `vertical_velocity` is a plain alias for `velocity.y`. Never reintroduce a stored scalar speed beside a separate direction.
   - ⚠️ **`current_speed` must stay read-only.** It was briefly settable, rescaling horizontal velocity in place to spare the call sites — but that is a magnitude abstraction over a vector, silently meaning "change my speed, keep my current direction". Correct for a crash, **wrong for a push**, with nothing marking the difference. It produced a real bug: pushing while drifting even slightly sideways scaled the sideways component up with the forward one, so a kick drove the skater along a crooked line — reported in play as the board "skidding sideways". Anything that changes motion must state what it does to the VECTOR (`_apply_push_impulse()`, `_kill_momentum()`).
   - **RULE:** A push drives along the **rolling axis**, never along the current travel vector, so it leaves the lateral component for grip to scrub and actively straightens a crooked roll. Fakie is not a special case: the axis is a line and the sign comes from which way the skater is already moving along it.
   - **RULE:** Grounded, `velocity.y` is pinned to zero and **height belongs entirely to the surface snap** — only the horizontal components are integrated. Airborne, `velocity.y` is the ballistic vertical speed. Do not integrate height twice.
   - **RULE:** Landing residuals are **transferred to the rig yaw**, never discarded. `board_pivot.y` carries only the $0/180^\circ$ switch-stance flip that every `cos(board_pivot.rotation_degrees.y)` test depends on. Naming quantises (`_finalise_trick_name()` rounds to half-turns) while physics stays continuous — a $185^\circ$ landing is still *named* a 180.
   - **RULE:** Outcomes that depend on "how sideways is this" are judged on **lateral momentum**, not angle. Tolerance then scales with speed for free, which is the whole point: the same $90^\circ$ landing must be survivable at walking pace and fatal at speed.
   - ⚠️ Wheel anisotropy (`wheel_side_grip`) is **one mechanism serving four behaviours** — imperfect-landing drift, the landing speed penalty, sideways wash-out, and the cost of carving. Retuning it moves all four. There is deliberately no separate "carve friction".
6. **Camera: Position and Aim Must Derive From the SAME Smoothed Yaw:**
   - **THE camera invariant.** `CameraPivot` originally *carried* the boom offset `(0, 1.25, 2.1)`, so it sat 2.1 m behind the skater and rotating it spun the camera on the spot — like turning your head while standing still. Rig yaw was inherited rigidly, so on an imperfect landing the camera's **position** whipped round instantly while only its **aim** was smoothed.
   - Measured on a $70^\circ$ landing: the skater sat **$60.8^\circ$ off the centre of view** — outside a $70^\circ$ FOV entirely — and took 1.2 s to return. ⚠️ **It did not present as a framing bug.** It read as "the pan feels too slow", and two rounds of rate tuning chased the wrong thing. The giveaway was the user reporting the board appearing *at the bottom of the screen*.
   - **RULE:** The boom lives on `Camera3D`; `CameraPivot` orbits the rig origin. Rotating it walks the camera **around** the skater while it keeps facing them. Never move the offset back up.
   - **RULE:** `ground_physics.gd` asserts the angle between camera forward and direction-to-skater stays within $8^\circ$ of its resting value on every landing. Verified to fail (5/9, $66.1^\circ$) with the boom restored to the pivot.
7. **Camera Chases TRAVEL, Not the Board's Heading:**
   - Roll up a bank and back down and gravity reverses your travel **without touching rig yaw** — only steering and the landing residual transfer call `rotate_y()`. A board-following camera therefore just sat there watching the skater come at it.
   - **RULE:** The camera tracks the direction of travel. This also gets fakie right for free: land a 180 and travel is unchanged, so the shot correctly does not move even though the board reversed.
   - ⚠️ **This DELETED machinery rather than adding it.** Board heading *jumps* at touchdown, which was the sole reason a spring-damped `_camera_jump_offset` existed to absorb and hand back those jumps. Travel direction never jumps — it realigns at a bounded rate through wheel grip — so the workaround went with its cause. Prefer removing the source of a discontinuity over damping it.
   - ⚠️ **The side view is TWO separate offsets, and they do different jobs.** `camera_side_offset_m` (0.26 m) is the visible one: it slides the viewpoint sideways while the aim stays along travel, so the vanishing point stays centred and the skater sits off-centre. `camera_side_offset_deg` (2.5°) *orbits* the camera, which rotates the AIM — at 18° that made the world stream diagonally and read as genuine distortion on a wide FOV, not merely an angle. Keep the orbit term small; it exists for the tie-break, not the look.
   - **RULE:** Switching sides smooths the **selection** (`_camera_side_smooth`, a continuous −1..+1), not the two offsets separately, so orbit and slide always move in step and cannot disagree part-way through a switch. Passing through zero sweeps the camera through dead centre, which is what makes the change read as a move rather than a cut.
   - **RULE:** `camera_side_offset_deg` is **not cosmetic** — it is what makes a reversal unambiguous, and the positional offset contributes nothing to it. Anchored to the **rig's lateral axis**, so the rider's sides do not move when gravity turns them round; the camera must therefore cross to the other side *relative to travel*, and the swing becomes $180^\circ - 2\phi$ one way against $180^\circ + 2\phi$ the other. Ordinary shortest-path smoothing then picks the direction on its own. **Set it to 0 and a dead-straight reversal becomes a true coin-flip** needing a tie-break rule invented for it.
   - **RULE:** Build the offset from the **perpendicular component** of the side vector, never a `sign()` test. Travelling exactly sideways, the perpendicular form fades the offset smoothly to nothing and returns it on the other side; a sign test flips on that boundary and makes the camera chatter.
   - ⚠️ Named by SIDE (`camera_side` = ±1), never frontside/backside: which one shows the rider's front depends on stance, so stance-relative names would mean opposite things for regular and goofy riders while the camera did exactly the same thing.
   - Heading is held below `camera_travel_min_speed` — the heading of a near-zero vector is noise, and that is exactly the state at the top of a bank. This is also what makes the swing wait until the skater is genuinely rolling back down.
8. **Camera: Continuous Tracking and Discontinuous Jumps Need Separate Rates:**
   - **RULE:** One rate cannot serve both. Fast enough that ground turns do not feel *boaty* is far too fast for a half-turn reversal; slow enough to pace the reversal makes every turn feel like steering a barge. `camera_follow_speed` converges on the goal; `camera_max_swing_deg` paces the **goal itself**.
   - **RULE:** Rate-limit the goal, then smooth toward it. The limiter turns a reversal into a swing rather than a snap; the lerp rounds off the start and stop a bare rate limit would leave as step changes in angular velocity. `camera_max_swing_deg` sits above the ~$172^\circ/\text{s}$ the board can yaw under full lean, so ordinary steering passes through untouched.
   - `camera_position_damp` follows the skater with **velocity fed forward**, so constant-speed riding has zero lag and the authored framing is untouched; only changes in motion let the camera fall behind. This is the seam to hang per-trick camera work off (grinds, manuals).
   - **RULE:** The same split applies to the **direction of travel**, and for the same reason. `landing_turn_rate_deg` caps how fast travel may swing while realigning from a touchdown; `wheel_side_grip` stays at full strength for carving, which is a steady state and needs it (at full lean the board yaws ~$172^\circ/\text{s}$). Both slews default to $60^\circ/\text{s}$ **so the world and the view turn in lockstep** — retune one and you must retune the other or they visibly disagree.
   - ⚠️ Cap the **angular rate**, never the lateral force. A force cap gets the shape backwards: scrubbing removes a fixed slice of lateral speed per frame, so as the remainder shrinks that same slice becomes an ever *larger* angle, and the worst jerk lands on the final frame of the settle. First attempt at this ramped grip in over 0.12 s and measured **2.32°** on its last frame versus 5.00° unsmoothed — better, but with the discontinuity merely moved. Capping the angle gives a flat $1.00^\circ$/frame.
   - ⚠️ **Impact absorption is not a fix for harsh landings.** `landing_dip_max` is polish only. A straight ollie has exactly the same dead-stop impact as a badly-rotated one, and straight ollies read as fine — so impact was never the complaint, the travel-direction jerk was. Diagnosis came from instrumenting every quantity across a touchdown rather than from tuning by feel.
   - ⚠️ The board's WORLD orientation is preserved exactly across a landing — the rig gains precisely what `board_pivot` gives up. If a landing ever looks like the deck jerks, that is a real bug, not tuning; `ground_physics.gd` asserts `boardjump == 0` on every landing case for exactly this reason, because "it feels abrupt" cannot distinguish a camera swing from a board jerk by eye.
9. **Never Let an Animation's DURATION Decide a Physical Outcome:**
   - This produced two separate bugs from one root cause (see Resolved, above): trick success gated on whether a fixed-duration flip had finished, and a tre flip's two axes retiring 11 frames apart. Both came from treating deck rotation as a set of independent timed animations rather than as one physical event.
   - **RULE:** Outcomes are judged on **state**, never on elapsed time or completion flags. Landability asks *where is the deck now* (`catch_cone_deg`), never *did the animation finish*. Anything that reads airtime, frame counts or animation duration to decide whether a trick worked is the same bug returning — a world change (ledge height, `jump_impulse`, `gravity_accel`, physics tick rate) must never be able to make a trick mathematically unlandable.
   - **RULE:** A trick is **one event**. When a trick drives more than one axis, scale every axis onto a single shared duration in `_impart_deck_rotation()` so they retire on the same frame. Never give two axes independent fixed rates.
   - **RULE:** Tolerances are **derived from geometry and materials**, not authored. `catch_cone_deg` is `min(atan(grip_friction), rail-strike angle)`, and the rail-strike angle is measured from the loaded deck via `SkateDeckMesh.deck_extents()` — decks are swappable, and a wider deck reaches the ground at a shallower angle. Do not replace either with a literal.
   - **RULE:** Never teleport the deck to its resting orientation. Integrate the rotation it already carried (`_advance_flip_settle()`); a snap of up to a full catch cone is exactly the visual pop the settle phase exists to prevent. Guarded by the step-size assertion in `curb_flip_repro.gd`.

## 🐛 Known Bugs
- ⚠️ **`_apply_airborne_board_pitch()` has no upper stick bound, but the touchdown manual zone does.** Airborne pitch uses `back.y > 0.15` with no ceiling, so full deflection lerps to the full $24^\circ$; the landing check at `_evaluate_touchdown_landing()` requires `back.y <= 0.90`. Holding the stick past `0.90` through a landing therefore falls out of the manual zone and trips `abs(pitch) > max_landing_tolerance_deg` → `BAIL! (Wheel Bite / Over-Pitched)`. `_apply_grounded_board_pitch()` already applies the `<= 0.90` ceiling — the airborne twin does not. Same "stall" symptom as the two rotation bugs below, different cause.
- ⚠️ **Rotation stops dead at the catch — no deceleration.** Both axes halt the instant they reach target. Physically defensible (the feet arrest the board, which really is near-instantaneous) and no longer *desynchronised*, but there is no ease-out. If catches still read abruptly in play, a short catch-decel over the last few degrees is the remaining refinement. Deliberately left alone as a feel decision.
- ⚠️ **Ollie-to-manual onto the Platform survives on `2 mm` of margin** — pop apex is `0.8022 m` against the `0.8 m` deck. Any downward tweak to `jump_impulse` or upward tweak to `gravity_accel` silently makes the Platform unreachable, and it will present as an unrelated regression.

### ✅ Resolved
- ~~**Flip tricks unlandable on any raised surface.**~~ Reported as "kickflip into a manual on the curb stalls every time"; the manual was incidental, a plain kickflip onto the curb bailed identically. `is_flip_in_progress` cleared only after the deck swept a full $360^\circ$ at `flip_speed_deg`, a **fixed 0.60 s wall-clock duration** regardless of hang time — while hang time *shrinks with the height gained from takeoff surface to landing surface*. Popping onto the `y = 0.30` curb gave 35 frames against 36 needed: one frame short, deterministically. `_evaluate_touchdown_landing()` hit the primo branch and `return`ed **before** the manual-zone check, which is why it presented as "flip tricks break manuals". Fixed by the orientation-based catch rule below.
- ~~**360-spin tricks finished their yaw ~11 frames before their roll.**~~ A tre flip's yaw ran at `spin_speed_deg * 2` (0.42 s) while its roll ran at `flip_speed_deg` (0.59 s), so the deck visibly stopped spinning at roughly $253^\circ$ of flip and kept rolling alone before halting. Same root cause as the above — independent fixed-rate animations with no shared notion of the trick as one event. Fixed by `_impart_deck_rotation()`.

Three separate ones surfaced in a single session (reversed ankle pegs, inverted shuv naming, inverted body-rotation naming), each found by *play*, not by tests, because each fails silently as a mirror image rather than an error.
